# What you throw away doesn’t disappear.

It ends up in our soil, our water and our neighbors’ lungs. Sorting waste correctly and handling household and campus chemicals safely are two small habits that protect the whole community.

[Sign the petition](#petition)[Learn to sort](#segregation)

## Four bins, one habit

Most communities and campuses use a color-coded system based on the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003). Colors can differ by barangay, school or building, so always follow the labels where you are.

### Green: biodegradable

Food scraps, fruit peels, leaves, paper towels, garden waste.

Can become compost.

### Blue: recyclable

Clean paper, cardboard, plastic bottles, cans, glass.

Rinse and dry first.

### Black: residual

Chip wrappers, used tissue, sachets, dirty packaging that can’t be recycled.

The last resort bin.

### Red: special and hazardous

Batteries, bulbs, old medicine, aerosol cans, e-waste, chemical containers.

Never mix with the others.

## Chemicals at home and on campus

Everyday cleaners and lab supplies can hurt people, drains and rivers when they are mixed, poured away or thrown in the wrong bin. Hazardous waste is covered by RA 6969, and your school’s environmental health and safety office or your local government can tell you where to take it.

**Never mix cleaning products.** Bleach with ammonia makes toxic chloramine gas. Bleach with vinegar, toilet cleaner or other acids releases chlorine gas. Use one product at a time, with the window open.

Common examples and safer handling

| Where | Item | Do this | Don’t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Bleach, toilet and drain cleaners | Keep in the original labeled bottle. Use up, or bring leftovers to a hazardous waste drop-off. | Mix products or pour large amounts into waterways. |
| Home | Batteries, power banks, phones | Collect in a sealed jar and bring to an e-waste or battery collection point. | Burn them or put them in the general bin. |
| Home | Fluorescent bulbs, thermometers | Handle gently; these may contain mercury. Take to special waste collection. | Crush or break them. |
| Home | Old medicine, aerosol cans, paint | Keep in the original container, away from children. Return to a pharmacy or collection event. | Flush medicine or burn aerosol cans. |
| Home | Used cooking oil | Cool, store in a bottle, and give to a collector or reuse program. | Pour down the sink. |
| University | Lab solvents, acids, bases | Sort by hazard type into labeled waste containers, as your lab instructor directs. | Pour down the sink or combine incompatible wastes. |
| University | Printer toner, ink, lab glassware, broken thermometers | Return toner to suppliers. Put broken glass in a rigid, labeled container. | Throw in the regular trash. |
| University | Empty chemical bottles | Ask the lab staff how to triple-rinse and label them. | Reuse them for food or drinks. |

This page is general guidance, not a substitute for the safety data sheet (SDS) of a product or your institution’s rules. If someone is exposed to fumes or swallows a chemical, call your local emergency number or poison control right away.
